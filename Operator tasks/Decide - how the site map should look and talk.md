# Decide: how the site map should look and talk

## What this is

The site map is the big visual canvas of the whole website: every page, what's on it, what's live, what's missing, and what's waiting on you. You don't like how it's turned out: the layout, the approach, and the way it talks. So instead of you editing it piece by piece, we'll build **five different versions** and you pick the one that's closest.

These 10 questions are what the five get built from. They're about **layout, approach and voice only**. No copy redlining.

- **How to answer:** write a letter, or **two letters if you're torn**. Torn is useful: that's exactly where the five versions split, so we test both. A few words after the letter help but aren't needed. An empty fence means not answered.
- **Time:** about 12 minutes.
- **What happens next:** five builders run in parallel on a fresh canvas (the current map stays as it is until you pick). Each builds the same slice: the overview of the whole site, the homepage, and Ivana's page. A blind judge ranks the five against your answers before you look, so you open the likely winner first. You pick one, or mix ("2's layout, 4's voice"), and the winner gets rolled out to every page.
- **Voice:** whichever voice you pick, it gets pulled from your own recordings (about 10 hours of you talking today), not written from scratch.
- When you're done, say "done" in the site-map chat.

# Q11.1 What's the map for, when you open it?

What should it show you first?

- (a) **The sitemap.** The whole site on one screen: every page, the menu, what links to what.
- (b) **The pages.** Each page drawn top to bottom, so you can judge its layout.
- (c) **The status.** Page by page: what's live, what's missing, what's waiting on you.
- (d) **The journeys.** What Ivana, a cold visitor or someone's AI agent sees, step by step.

My recommendation: (a) on top, with (c) as colours on it, and (b) one zoom-level down. It's the only way to see everything and the gaps at once.
