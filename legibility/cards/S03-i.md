## Gist

Calling it one of his spiciest takes (the set-up is in the context line before), Alex lays out the three tribes of AI: skilled practitioners today mostly came from academia, from coding, or from entrepreneurship. Academics (machine learning people, computer scientists) saw AI first, still see it as fairly abstract and are the least hands-on with commercial AI, but in organisations they know the security side. Former developers are strong on the deterministic toolkit, and coding turned out useful, though less required than they or the academics believe and more than marketers think; the third tribe is entrepreneurs, many of whom are marketers.

## Points

- His "three tribes of AI": skilled AI practitioners right now probably came from one of three backgrounds.
- Academics (machine learning people, computer scientists) saw AI first and still see it as quite abstract.
- Academics are the least hands-on with commercial AI, but in organisations they really know the security part.
- Former developers came to AI from coding and are really good at the deterministic toolkit: reading and writing code.
- Coding turned out to be useful, but less required than developers or academics believe, and more required than marketers think.
- The third tribe is entrepreneurs, and a lot of entrepreneurs are marketers; why that is comes after the moment ends (context lines).
