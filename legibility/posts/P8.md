## Post 1 · Update · at 17:13

### Alex stops everything for a reset

At 16:44, Alex halted every agent. Too many of them sat waiting on him for answers that, once he read them, didn't need him.

> "There's too much stuff that's like paused on me, and I read through it and it's dumb. So gently halt anything that's not actively working on something serious."
> — Alex, S10, 16:44

By 16:49 every session had paused and saved its work. Alex then went through the backlog with the agent that coordinates the others, and set new rules for all of them:

- **Easy calls belong to the agents.** They decide, write down why, and carry on. Alex can change anything later.
- **No more plan approvals.** Alex signs off only what the finish line names: the six key people, the brand on the templates and LinkedIn posts, and the last touches on each blog post.
- **Public information is fair game.** The agents stop asking him about it. Secrets and a short list of private items stay off.
- **Writing goes into shared documents** that Alex edits when he has a minute, so nothing waits on him.
- **No 18:00 cut-off.** The build runs through the night.

> "Please unstuck everything. If things are an easy judgment, please clear the plate for me. We can just make edits later."
> — Alex, S10, 16:56

The waiting approvals were closed on their defaults, and everything that still needs Alex now sits in one list. The agents restarted at 17:13, several of them as fresh sessions picking up from a written handover.

## Post 2 · Insight · at 16:58

### "The bottleneck is always human"

Fourteen minutes after the halt, Alex named what had gone wrong. The agents could take on more work. He couldn't answer them fast enough.

> "The bottleneck is always, always, always human. It's not the amount of stuff that the agents can focus on, it's humans, and I'm paying the price for jumping in."
> — Alex, S10, 16:58

His fix for next time: spend longer on the plan before anything runs, so the agents need to ask less.

> "I should have not executed a single thing to like two o'clock. I should have just been writing specifically what I wanted for each thing."
> — Alex, S10, 16:58

In his 17:07 check-in, he put it another way: people have a context window too.

> "Humans have like RAM too … there's a human context window … Either you decide you're offloading to the AI, or you have to keep the velocity at a certain amount. You can get a little bit more leverage if you really take the time to write good guiding documents."
> — Alex, S11, 17:09, link: https://youtu.be/iXr-pKhseB8?t=160

He said much the same at 12:28, before the agents started: "The bottleneck is focus." Four and a half hours later, he acted on it.
