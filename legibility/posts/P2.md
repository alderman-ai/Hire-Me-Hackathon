## Timeline

### 10:25 · S01 · Recording starts

Alex switches on a recording rig he built the night before and starts with the first thing followers need: this website. The code is in a public repo from the first commit.

> "I've never done this screen recording before, but good time to start is when you're under pressure and you've got a 12-hour live project to do."
> — S01 at 00:08 · [watch](https://youtu.be/mbF291oW_yQ?t=3)

### 11:06 · S02 · The explainer

The video at the top of this page: why a GTM engineer, why build it live, and the idea behind it all, knowledge arbitrage. Personalized outreach used to cost days of work per account. Now an agent can do that work in the background, and most people don't know it yet.

> "There's going to be a space for so good AI that it looks human and looks impossible for it to be AI. That's the niche. So good that they think you spent three days building it just for them, when an agent was running in the background for a couple hours."
> — S02 at 08:50 · [watch](https://youtu.be/qrBNOsSYGrQ?t=525)

### 11:25 · S03 · Writing the plan every agent works from

Alex writes the project charter with Claude: the goal, the deliverables, and the rules every agent follows. The rules include the movie-actor rule (everything that needs him gets done first) and one "answer desk" window for all of the agents' questions. In between, he takes a break to talk to the humans watching.

> "The progress of learning AI is not the files you have in your computer. It's the clarity of understanding of how all this stuff works … it's clarity. And then the opposite of that is the ability to clearly share that clarity with the assistant."
> — S03 at 18:10

### 12:15 · S04 · The deliverables, and why go slow at the start

Alex lists the ten demos you see above. One of them is an agent that watches these recordings and keeps this website up to date, which is how this section got here. He also explains why he spends the first hours planning instead of building.

> "Mistakes cascade and they just snowball. You really want to take the time to get it right, because time is no longer the bottleneck, because you can have 20 agents running side by side. The bottleneck is focus. The bottleneck is judgment, discernment."
> — S04 at 12:55

### 12:40 · S05 · The agents take over, and one big mistake

The plan moves from the chat app to the terminal. An orchestration session takes the charter. Three agents write the three tooling specs in parallel, and the answer desk opens. Alex answers each spec's opening questions in one batch, so the agents can carry on without him.

Then, at 13:20, he tries to close one background session he no longer needs, and closes all of them instead.

> "I just accidentally ended a bunch of sessions, background sessions, they're all working in parallel. I meant to just delete one I didn't need, and turns out if you do that command on the folder, it deletes all of them."
> — S05 at 41:31

**How it was recovered.** A recovery session brought every session back by about 13:27, each under its old name with its full history. Each one got a note saying exactly where it had stopped and what to pick up. The recording never stopped (the rig and both recorders survived), and nothing that had been committed was lost. Because every session commits its work as it goes, the rest of the project carried on. The recovery session is now turning the fix into a reusable skill, so the next time (a power cut, another wrong click) it's one step.

> "Hopefully it can spin up all the sessions that were working and just resume, and this is just a 15-minute setback and not an hour-and-a-half setback."
> — S05 at 44:24. It was about seven minutes.

### 13:33 · S06 · Status update

A two-minute check-in: the plan is written, and the agents are breaking it into pieces they can build on their own.

> "At the beginning, it's going to look like nothing's happening behind the scenes. AI just is very front-loaded … and then basically everything happens all at once, in parallel."
> — S06 at 00:03

### 13:45 · The demos get their folders

The bootstrap agent creates a working folder for every demo. The agents that build them launch next.

### 13:49 · The first tooling piece is done

Bootstrap finishes. Every piece of work now has its folder, its instructions and its place on the session roster, and bootstrap hands over to the orchestration session, which launches the rest.
