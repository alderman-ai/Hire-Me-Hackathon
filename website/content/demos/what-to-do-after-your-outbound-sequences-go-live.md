"I expected this one to have the best results. I'm getting bad results."

A lot of outbound gets treated like a one-time setup: write the sequences, load the list, press play. **Nothing in AI is a one-time setup and press play.** Building is a phase. It's not the deliverable. Refining is the main work, and that's my job as a GTM engineer: finding out which sequences actually work, killing the ones that don't, and getting the cost of each prospect down.

## Predict the winner, then check

Say you've got 120 sequences: different customer types, languages, even personalities, because anyone good at sales mirrors people. It doesn't matter how many you have. It matters who gets put into which one.

So for everyone on the list, I ask a classification model which sequence should get the best results. It's an AI tool that doesn't write anything. It gives you odds.

Then the results come in, and you build up a record of exactly that sentence: **expected the best results, getting bad results.** That gives you two moves. Tweak how the model decides. Or kill the sequence that's always worse than the others.

That's what I mean by systems that refine themselves through use.

## For key accounts, watch mutual connections

That loop runs on volume. Key accounts need patience. My niche has always been B2B tech services, with buying cycles of a year or more, where you need advocates inside a company to tell you when the RFP is coming.

Before you go after any decision maker, **the number one metric is how many mutual connections you've got.** In my own industry, anytime I talk to someone, I've got 50-plus. You can simulate that.

Search for the 200 least senior people connected to the person you actually want, and run a connection campaign on them. No sequence, just connect. Juniors accept more than other people. 200 sends is maybe a month on one account. Then when you message the person you're after, you've got 50 mutual connections.

Meanwhile, keep up on the account's news every week: new hires, new positions. When someone new lands in a role you care about, don't bother them when they first get there. They're busy. Find their job advert instead. It almost always spells out their 90-day goals, so you know what they're working on and when the timing is right.

## Then get the cost down

The target is one to three or four euros a person, about 98% automated, where whoever gets it thinks they're one of your top one or two accounts, because no one else would put in that much effort.

Honestly, I'm not there yet. Right now it's still high cost: what you're looking at was a 12-hour hackathon. My goal is to get it under five euros in a few months.

## Refining is the main work

> [!video] _vgNB5ubKjA 883 926
> Building is a phase, not the deliverable: refining is the main work, and your salespeople have to be able to run it

Maintaining it. Looking at the data. Constantly asking: is our approach working? What do we need to switch? It's a systems management job, not a type-things-into-AI job, and it has to be built so your salespeople, who aren't AI experts, can run it.

So when the sequence I expected to win is always worse than the others, I don't argue with it. I kill it.
