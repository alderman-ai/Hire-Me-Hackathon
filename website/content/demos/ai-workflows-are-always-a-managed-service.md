A lot of AI gets sold like this: "We'll build you this workflow. You pay us, we build it, transaction over, and it works like software."

That's a lie. An AI workflow is never a product. **It's always a managed service.** Someone has to keep it running, and someone has to get your people to actually use it.

That's the job I do. **I set up AI workflows for a team as a service that keeps working:** I build them, I teach your people to run them, or we mix the two, and I stay with you as the models and tools change underneath.

## Nothing stays the same for six months

When Opus 5.5 came out, it completely redid how skills work in Claude Code, the AI tool I build in. Skills are the saved instructions a workflow runs on. A lot of the skills written before it no longer work the way they're supposed to, because the model changed and so did the whole idea of how a skill should work and how the model uses one.

New models come out from all the labs every month or two, and it doesn't take a change that big to break a workflow.

The model is only one side of it, right? Your ICP shifts and you need to send something completely different. A new law passes, and something that was fine yesterday is a violation today.

> [!quote]
> The fact that anything would be the same in 3 to 6 months is something that you should seriously question.
> Alex Alderman, 3 Nov 2026

## The bottleneck is always people

I've worked in software adoption, and the bottleneck is never the software. It's people, people, people.

Say I build something for your sales team. The easiest a workflow can possibly be is one slash command: a single typed line, and the whole thing runs. Even then, they have to remember to type it, and be willing to go to the screen where they type it. That's already a bar to clear, and clearing it takes some training.

> [!figure] /demos-assets/ai-workflows-are-always-a-managed-service/slash-command.png
> one slash command typed into a terminal, with a whole workflow behind that single line
> That's the whole interface: one typed line. Your people still have to remember to type it

And they need to know it well enough that the friction doesn't make them decide never to use it. A workflow nobody opens is money spent on nothing.

The marketers say a good build is wizard magic: anyone can use it, no skill gap. Okay, cool, bro, but there is one. It's not a 4-hour SAP course. (Those 3-hour AI fluency courses? I don't know who they're for, but it's not people learning AI.)

**Teaching AI is so little about AI.** It's getting people who aren't super interested in change (most people), aren't excited about AI (most people) and aren't excited about their work radically changing (most people) to learn something new anyway.

> [!quote]
> To make it engaging and get people willing to learn something new is like 2% AI and 98% teaching, which is where my teaching background comes in handy.
> Alex Alderman, 3 Nov 2026

## You pick the spot between building and teaching

> [!video] LnXzjUVoVpU
> The choice every company has to make: use what others build, maintain it, or build it yourself

Every setup sits somewhere on a slider. At the bottom, I do most of the building. Slide up, and I build less while your team learns more.

> [!diagram] scale
> The build-teach slider: higher up, the vendor builds less and your company learns more
> 1. Zero building: the vendor only teaches, and your team builds it all
> 2. Build: build it together, or the vendor gets it running so it runs while you learn
> 3. Use and maintain: the vendor builds it and teaches your team to use and fix it
> 4. Use: the vendor builds it, trains your team, and maintains it or says honestly it could break
> 5. Zero teaching: the vendor builds and nobody learns, which is the pitch, and it doesn't work

**At the low end, I build it, train your team to use it, and stay on a retainer.** I fix the pipeline when something changes, train new people as they join, and refresh the training when it's needed. If you don't want a retainer, I'll tell you straight that it could break, and that's just the way it is.

Slide up, and you say: no ongoing costs, just teach us how to fix it. That's possible. But fixing it is a lot more complicated than most people think, so a lot of that retainer cost just moves into training.

Whatever the blend, I build it differently than I'd build it for myself. Usually that's a core workflow with maybe three levels of complexity, and one honest question: where can we get you in two weeks?

Most companies need a bit more information before they choose, because the first instinct is "just teach us, what is it, a 3-hour course?" It's not a 3-hour course.

## Never make the contractor the line manager

Wherever you land, one job stays yours. People resist change, and any change to how a company works needs management and line managers to carry it.

> [!quote]
> If you're ever in a position where you're trying to make an external vendor a line manager to your people, you've done something wrong.
> Alex Alderman, 3 Nov 2026

Transformation has to be part of the whole company: leadership bought in, and the change built into hiring and performance reviews. If you're not there yet, that's fine. But a contractor playing line manager won't work, and the contractor probably won't enjoy the work much either.

> [!figure] /demos-assets/ai-workflows-are-always-a-managed-service/who-owns-the-change.png
> who owns the change: leadership and line managers above the team, the vendor beside it, building and teaching
> Leadership and line managers carry the change. The vendor sits beside your team, building and teaching

## Harder than they'll tell you

So before anyone builds you a workflow, pick your spot on the slider knowingly, and get two answers: who fixes it when something changes, and how much teaching your team needs to actually use it.

For reference, I've been on an AI sabbatical for 9 months, in Claude Code 40 to 50 hours a week. Does your team need 9 months? No. But it's harder than you think, and harder than the AI marketers will tell you.

And yes, you can totally trust me on that. I'm an AI marketer.
