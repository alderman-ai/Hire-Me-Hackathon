Everyone's blasting out AI right now: low quality, high volume. **There's going to be a space for AI so good that it looks human,** so good it looks impossible for it to be AI. That's the niche, and the price of entry is sounding like a person.

## Effort used to be the signal

People always knew when they were being shotgun blasted. And they knew when someone had taken the time to make it personal. That was the whole point of personal outreach: it was a costly signal. You couldn't fake the hours.

This site is me paying for that signal the old way. Twelve hours building in public, on camera, for one company, then probably the evening and some of the next morning. That's legitimately costly, and it's on camera, so you know it's costly.

I can't do it for 30 companies a day.

Yet.

## Now the effort is cheap, and the signal isn't

Once the automations are built, it looks costly, and it isn't.

> [!quote]
> So good that they think you spent three days building it just for them when an agent was running in the background for a couple hours.
> Alex Alderman, on camera during the hackathon, 2 Nov 2026

**That gap is worth money.** People don't know yet that this level of customization is possible through automations, so it still reads as three days of someone's attention. The flood of low-quality AI makes it stand out even more, because everything around it is so obviously blasted.

## "Looks human" is the hard part

Getting an agent to do the research and fill in the fields is the easy bit. Sounding like a person is where it falls over.

I know, because it happened on this exact build. The agents wrote the site's copy and the notes to the people I was writing to, and I hated almost all of it.

> [!quote]
> The tone of reaching out to people is just so weird and unnatural, and this weird mix of super formal and really awkward, and that's not me.
> Alex Alderman, in a message to his AI agents, 2 Nov 2026

That's what AI writing does when nobody's captured the taste. A machine optimizes for semantic correctness. A human optimizes for what the eye resolves at skim speed.

**So we fixed it,** and not with a prompt or a style guide gathering dust. The transcripts of me talking out loud are a treasure trove, so every word on this site is now drafted from them and from my own posts. Then a separate grader with no memory of the draft passes or fails it against my writing rubric. This article went through that loop, and I still do the last 10% myself.

That's the bar for anything that goes out under someone's name: it has to sound like them. In outreach, that's the difference between a message that gets deleted and one that gets a reply.

I have too much pride for slop. So I guess we're going to have to build it right.
