- [ ] R1 Making the work legible. How do the strongest GTM-engineer portfolios, hackathon demos and "job application as outreach" sites make the work legible to a non-technical decision maker in under a minute? Is there a converged pattern (short video, live build log, before/after, a "how it works" panel)? What makes such demos read as toys, or as noise? · serves: "legibility matters more than function"; D8 "keeps getting clearer about what is being built" · tools: WebSearch, web-fetch · deep: no
- [ ] R2 What a GTM engineer is hired to show. What do agencies and B2B companies hiring GTM engineers in 2026 look for: skills, tool stacks, proof of work? Which of the ten deliverables map to that, and what does a hiring manager expect that none of them shows? · serves: the outcome ("demonstrates what a GTM engineer can do"); every D-spec's framing · tools: WebSearch, web-fetch (job posts and practitioner writing, not Addvery's) · deep: no
- [ ] R3 Personal data on a public site. What do GDPR and LinkedIn's terms allow when a project scrapes public information about six named people, publishes a section about each on a public website, and runs them through a live outbound sequence from the operator's own account? What's the converged safe practice for personalised outreach of this kind, and what should stay off the site? · serves: Scope (the D3 live run), D8 stakeholder sections, the risks rule · tools: WebSearch, web-fetch (regulator guidance, case law, LinkedIn's published terms) · deep: no
- [ ] R4 Does the format land? Is there evidence (case studies, recruiter and founder accounts) on how recipients react to a personalised website built as a job application? What tips it from impressive to creepy or self-indulgent, and what makes the recipient act on it? · serves: the outcome ("works as a highly personalized outreach job application") · tools: WebSearch, web-fetch · deep: no
