# How the posts were made

The method comes from Alex's public repo `brand-voice-capture-demo-linkedin`. It was applied to his capture of Addvery's public voice in four steps.

1. **Capture.** Addvery's public voice became 11 brand rules: four hard don'ts (no corporate filler, no pep talk, no AI hype, no invented results or real names) and seven voice rules (open on something you can count, tell it like the people who did the work, short sentences, one dry aside, light emphasis, AI in delivery terms, the result in business words). They sit on top of 10 LinkedIn basics from Alex's public rubric: clean formatting, a hook that fits above the "see more" cut and invites the click, no generic AI-essay register. One rule set has a writing view and a grading view. Hard rules come first, and line lengths are counted by a script, never eyeballed.
2. **Write.** One AI writer per post, hook first, checked by the script before handing in.
3. **Judge.** A separate AI judge that wrote none of the posts scored each one blind against all 21 rules, with the posts relabelled and shuffled and a generic post mixed in. Misses went back for a rewrite: three rounds, then a brand-review pass, then a fresh judge on the final set. All five posts met all 21 rules.
4. **Sign-off.** Alex reads them and decides. AI drafted under his direction; he approves.

## What round 1 caught

In round 1, the judge found all five writers had copied the rule sheet's own example lines. Each post looked fine alone; side by side they read like a template. So the rules gained set-level checks: no two posts may share an opener, a click line, an aside or an ending.

## The contrast

The same AI model, asked for a post on post 1's topic in Addvery's brand voice, with Addvery's public tagline and no rules, met 12 of 21 (`baseline.md`). A one-line prompt without the brand voice met 10.

## Honest limits

- The score shows the posts follow the 21 rules. It isn't proof that they sound like Addvery to Addvery's own team: that takes a person from Addvery reading them.
- Alex's own rubric was induced from posts he graded and tested in about 1,560 blind judgments. This brand layer comes from a short capture of Addvery's public voice, with no graded Addvery posts behind it. It's a directional check, not a validated one.
- The register follows how Addvery's leaders write (short sentences, one dry aside), on the company page.
- Every number in the posts is illustrative, framed as how such projects usually go. None is a client result.
