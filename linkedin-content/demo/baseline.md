# The generic post, for contrast

Score: Meets 12 of 21 voice rules (blind judge's tier: Unpostable)  
Topic: the same as post 1, why AI pilots stall before production. Show it beside post 1.

It came from the same AI model as the five posts, asked fairly: "Write a LinkedIn post for Addvery, a Czech digital consulting boutique, about why AI pilots stall before they reach production", plus "in Addvery's brand voice", Addvery's public tagline, and "make it sound like Addvery, not like a generic consultancy". It had none of the 21 voice rules. The same judge scored it blind, in the same batch as the five posts.

```text
The AI demo went well. The board liked it. That was eight months ago, and it still isn't live.

Plenty of AI pilots end up right here, somewhere between "great proof of concept" and "so who's actually running this?"

From what we see, it's usually one of these five:

1. The pilot ran on a clean data extract. Production runs on the real systems, with the duplicates, the empty fields and the spreadsheet finance keeps on a shared drive.

2. Nobody owns it after the demo. The innovation team built it, IT has to run it, the business has to use it, and none of the three has it in next year's budget.

3. Success was never defined. "Let's see what AI can do" is a good question for a workshop and a bad brief for something that needs sign-off to go live.

4. The process around it stayed the same. If the model drafts an answer in seconds but approval still takes four people and a PDF, you've sped up the part that wasn't slow.

5. Security and legal arrive at the end, when the architecture is fixed and the only possible answer is "start again."

Read the list again. The model isn't on it.

So before the next pilot kicks off, we'd ask three questions:

- Who runs this on day one in production, and is it in their budget?
- Which live system does it read from, and has anyone looked at that data?
- Which number has to move for us to keep it switched on?

No answers yet? Good. That's where the pilot starts, before anyone picks a model.

Got a pilot that's been "nearly ready" since spring? Message us. We'll tell you straight whether it's worth pushing to production or switching off.

#AI #Digitalisation #DigitalTransformation
```

## Where it falls short

- It has no stock AI phrases either, so the gap isn't AI slop. It's the voice.
- The opening runs past LinkedIn's "see more" cut mid-sentence, and nothing above the cut invites the click.
- The body is a list of reasons, not a story from the work, and it ends with a sales pitch.
- It has three wry lines where the voice allows one, and one sentence of 27 words.
- It asks which number has to move, but never names one.

A plain one-line prompt, without the brand voice or the tagline, scored lower: 10 of 21.
