The five Addvery LinkedIn templates from this hackathon are still images. I'm adding motion in two pieces: a 5-second animation of one graphic, to post instead of the static image, and a one-minute brand video. I make both myself, by hand, in ComfyUI.

> [!quote]
> It's just to show that it's possible to add some energy to the brand through video generation.
> Alex Alderman, in a message to his AI agents, 2 Nov 2026

> - Seven ideas came first: four for the post, three for the minute.

## Where the ideas started

Each template is a signed-off image that sits still in the feed. When I asked my AI agents for ideas, I described this:

> [!quote]
> Instead of just having a static image on LinkedIn, we can have a light shimmer. We can have the brand getting some kind of 3D texture to it. It kind of builds itself.
> Alex Alderman, in a message to his AI agents, 2 Nov 2026

Two of the seven ideas take their names from that message: Light shimmer and It builds itself.

## Seven ideas to pick from

Four are for the 5-second post, three for the minute.

The brainstorm recommends Glass panel and Five posts, one light. Glass panel needs one pair of stills and one clip, and its text sits clear of the moving panel. Five posts, one light gets most of its stills free, from the backdrops and the 5-second post.

## Keeping the lettering sharp

Video models smear lettering. On a brand graphic, the words have to stay exact.

So the motion runs on each template's backdrop: the brand art with every text slot hidden. The sharp text goes back on top afterwards, as native Canva text over the clip or masked in from the original export.

## A one-minute brand video

The minute uses the method of a 30-second brand film I made earlier. In ComfyUI, I make a chain of stills with image edits, then a short first-to-last video clip between each pair, and cut the clips together.

That film took 13 or 14 stills, so a minute needs about twice as many, or longer holds.

The frame comes first: that film was 16:9, while the graphics are 4:5, 1:1 and 1.91:1. 16:9 suits this site, square or 4:5 the LinkedIn feed.
