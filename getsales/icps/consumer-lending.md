# Consumer lending: workflow copy

**Needs a native read.** The Czech and German copy was drafted without a native speaker; have one read every cell before anything is sent.

**Copy pass (2 Nov, marketing:email-sequence + marketing:brand-review):**
- **Sequence.** The signal message 1 examples no longer make follow-up 1's "now is the cheapest moment" point, and the signal briefs now leave it to follow-up 1. The problem-led follow-up 1 now names its own problem, and the English and German proof-led follow-up 1 no longer assumes message 1 named Home Credit, so both read right whichever side message 1 took. The proof-led message 1 now uses only Security Bank's five-products line, so follow-up 2 adds new detail (23 data sources, the 40+ page blueprint) instead of repeating it.
- **Consistency.** Every follow-up now signs off with `{{sender_first_name}}`, as in the other ICP files; before, only the signal follow-up 2s did.
- **Facts.** The Home Credit line now says "lead to signed contract" (the site's lead-to-contract reporting), not "first click". "One answer per applicant" is now "the same answer for the same applicant on every product". All three briefs now forbid promising any approval, risk or compliance outcome.
- **Language.** Czech: "dva žadatelé se stejným profilem" replaces "dva stejní žadatelé", plus a smoother timing line and "z opačného konce". German: "Ist Ihnen das 20 Minuten wert?" (was "Sind Ihnen das…"), "zwei identische Anträge", and the "Wenn es hilft" calque dropped. English: the problem-led question and "if either side is live" made plain.

ICP: consumer lenders and retail banks, from lead funnel to contract and credit decisioning. Languages CS, DE, EN; three approaches each; 9 workflows. Every workflow runs: connection request (unless already connected), message 1 a day after acceptance (or at once), follow-up 1 three days later, follow-up 2 three days after that. Any reply stops the sequence. Message 1 is custom message 1, written per lead before import from the brief below.

## Notes for the reader

- **Proof used, only as Addvery's website states it, and always placed in the Philippines:**
  - Home Credit Philippines: lifecycle CRM redesigned on the existing stack (four lifecycle stages, five use cases, lead-to-contract reporting); "Up to +30% projected increase in submitted leads"; "No new platform was needed".
  - Security Bank (Philippines): one approval design for five credit products; "23 data sources reviewed"; a "40+ page requirements blueprint" for loan origination system vendors. These are outputs, so the copy claims no outcome for them. Message 1 uses only the five-products line; follow-up 2 adds the rest, so it doesn't repeat message 1.
- **Honesty about geography.** Both of Addvery's lending cases are in the Philippines. Every line that uses them says so, and the proof-led follow-up 2 says it outright rather than leaving the reader to find out.
- **Czech greetings carry no name.** A variable can't put a first name into the vocative, and the nominative reads wrong in Czech, so the Czech copy opens with "Dobrý den," alone. German and English use {{first_name}}.
- **Company name never declines.** In Czech `{{company_name}}` only ever follows "ve společnosti"; client brands stay as the site writes them.
- **Sender's gender unknown.** Czech copy uses the present or future tense for the sender and "we" (jsme) for past work, never a first-person past-tense verb.
- **Sign-off.** Message 1 carries no signature; both follow-ups end with `{{sender_first_name}}` on its own line.
- **German uses Sie throughout** and opens with "Hallo {{first_name}},". "Projected" is "prognostiziert" or "Prognose", "up to" is "bis zu".
- **No Slovak copy.** Slovak leads get the Czech workflows.
- **Two sides of one ICP.** Leads may sit in CRM and marketing (the funnel) or in credit risk (the approval). Message 1 briefs pick the side by role; the follow-ups cover both, funnel first, approval in follow-up 2. Follow-up 1 names its own problem or case, so it reads right whichever side message 1 took. Lending is regulated: no line promises an approval rate, a risk result or a compliance outcome.
- **Example leads and companies are fictional** (Microsoft's sample company names: Woodgrove, Contoso, Fabrikam, Northwind).

## Czech (CS)

### Demo · Consumer lending · CS · signal

**Message 1 brief:** Rest the line on one dated, public trigger at the lead's company from the last 90 days: a new credit product or buy-now-pay-later launch, a loan origination system (LOS) selection or tender, a CRM or marketing-automation rollout, a new CRO, CMO or Head of Retail Lending, or hiring for scoring or CRM roles. Name the trigger plainly with its month, say in one clause why it reopens either the path from lead to signed contract or the approval rules across products (whichever fits the trigger and the lead's role), and end with one light question. Leave the timing argument (the rules get rewritten anyway, so now is the cheapest moment) to follow-up 1. Do not congratulate, guess at internal problems or numbers, cite any client or result (the follow-ups carry the proof), promise any approval, risk or compliance outcome, use private or personal data, list services, add a link or ask for a call. Addvery's voice: direct, plain, a little bold, never corporate. Length: a greeting plus 2-3 sentences, under 400 characters in all, no exclamation marks. Write in Czech with vykání. Open with 'Dobrý den,' and no name, since a name in the vocative is easy to get wrong. The sender's gender is unknown, so use the present tense ('vidím', 'čtu') or 'we' ('jsme'), never 'všiml jsem si'.

> Dobrý den, ještě poznámka k načasování. Nový produkt, nový systém nebo nové vedení znamená, že se pravidla stejně přepisují: který lead se počítá, kdo dostane jakou zprávu, jak se schvaluje žádost.
