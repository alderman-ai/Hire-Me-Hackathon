# Retail loyalty: workflow copy

Status: draft, **needs a native read** (Czech and German above all).

ICP: multi-market grocery and e-commerce retailers with loyalty programmes, in Central and Eastern Europe and German-speaking Europe: grocery, drugstore and online-retail chains. Buyers: CMO, Head of CRM or CVM, Head of Loyalty, Head of E-commerce, CDO. Czech, German and English × signal, problem and proof: 9 workflows. These are Addvery's messages to Addvery's prospects, sent by an Addvery person.

## How every workflow runs

| Step | When | Text |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Message 1 | 1 day after they accept, or at once if already connected | the lead's custom message 1, written before import to the brief below |
| Follow-up 1 | 3 days later | fixed text below |
| Follow-up 2 | 3 days after that | fixed text below: the last touch, with an easy way out |

Any reply stops the sequence.

## Conventions

- Message 1 opens with a greeting and carries no signature; both follow-ups sign off with `{{sender_first_name}}`.
- Czech greets without a name ("Dobrý den,"). `{{first_name}}` would land in the nominative, where Czech needs the vocative ("Petře", not "Petr"). `{{company_name}}` sits only after "ve společnosti", so it never has to decline. Client brands stay undeclined ("pro REWE Group", "pro BILLA CZ a SK").
- German opens with "Hallo {{first_name}}," and uses Sie throughout, the usual LinkedIn register in German-speaking Europe.
- Formal address throughout (vykání, Sie). No gendered first-person past tense in Czech: Addvery speaks as "we" ("jsme pracovali", "jsme převzali"), the sender only in the present or future ("vidím", "přestanu se ozývat").
- The light ask is 20 minutes or "worth a look?"; every Follow-up 2 ends with an easy no.

## Proof used, as Addvery's site states it

- **Rohlík Group**, "one of Europe's fastest-growing online grocers": customers arrived faster than a CRM team could be hired. Addvery took "interim ownership" of CRM, built behavioural segments, automated onboarding, churn and cross-sell journeys, rolled out to Czechia, Hungary, Austria and Germany, then handed over. Results: "+17% revenue per customer (ARPU)"; "4 countries on one CRM model".
- **REWE Group (BILLA, IKI)**: a "2M+ loyalty base" on a card that "knows everyone and no one", and CRM teams at BILLA CZ and SK "wanting execution capacity, not just recommendations". Addvery mapped "24+ shopping missions", took interim CRM ownership at BILLA CZ and SK, and A/B-tested the channels. Results: "+2% in-store revenue"; "+5% email open rate"; "4 markets (CZ, SK, BG, LT)".

Both cases report results, not projections, so no "projected" or "modelled" qualifier applies. The REWE work was in CZ, SK, BG and LT: the German copy never places it in Austria or Germany. Left out: the in-app click-through rate, the loyalty blueprint, the Küchen Quelle case (a considered purchase, not loyalty) and the placeholder testimonial.

Proof-led cells: message 1 cites REWE Group for chains with stores and a card, Rohlík Group for online grocers. In Czech and German, Follow-up 1 tells the REWE story and Follow-up 2 the Rohlík one; in English it's the other way round, since English leads are more often multi-market online groups. A lead whose message 1 cited the other case hears it again in Follow-up 2, this time as a story.

Leads at REWE Group, BILLA, IKI or Rohlík Group are the clients in these stories: route them by hand, not into these workflows.

Example leads are fictional: the routing sample's companies (Northwind Grocery CZ, Wingtip Markt AT, Proseware Online Grocery) and made-up first names.

## Czech (CS)

### Demo · Retail loyalty · CS · signal

**Message 1 brief** (for the LLM that writes custom message 1)

Write in Czech with formal vykání. Start with 'Dobrý den,' and no name (a first name would need the vocative), then 2-3 short sentences, under 400 characters in all. Rest it on the lead's one dated public trigger from the last 90 days: a CRM, loyalty or e-commerce hire or job post, a new CMO or CDO, a loyalty card or app relaunch, entry into a new country, or a move to a new marketing-automation platform. Name it plainly and only as the source states it; say in one sentence why that moment matters for the loyalty programme (it decides whether the card's data turns into offers that fit each customer, or stays a discount tool); end on one short, genuine question. Do not congratulate or flatter, pitch Addvery, cite clients or numbers (the follow-ups carry the proof), ask for a meeting, or use anything private. No gendered first-person past tense ('všiml/a jsem si') and no 'rád/a': use the present ('vidím') or 'we'. No links, emoji or exclamation marks.

**Message 1 example** (233 characters). Fictional lead: Head of CRM at Northwind Grocery CZ (Czech grocery chain); trigger: a job post for a CRM manager, 6 Oct 2026.

```text
Dobrý den, vidím, že Northwind Grocery CZ hledá CRM manažera. V téhle fázi bývá věrnostní karta plná dat, ale tým ještě nemá kapacitu udělat z nich komunikaci, která dává smysl každému zákazníkovi. Jak to řešíte, než se místo obsadí?
```

**Follow-up 1** (3 days later, 536 characters)

```text
Dobrý den,

proč píšu právě teď: nový člověk v CRM, nová aplikace nebo vstup na nový trh jsou chvíle, kdy se rozhoduje, k čemu bude věrnostní program svá data používat několik dalších let. Když se to odloží, zůstane karta hlavně slevovým nástrojem.

Jeden příklad: pro REWE Group (BILLA, IKI) jsme pracovali s věrnostní základnou přes 2 miliony členů ve čtyřech zemích, včetně Česka a Slovenska. Tržby v prodejnách vzrostly o 2 %.

Stálo by za 20 minut probrat, jak by to vypadalo ve společnosti {{company_name}}?

{{sender_first_name}}
```

**Follow-up 2** (3 days after that, 298 characters)

```text
Dobrý den,

tohle je moje poslední zpráva. Pokud se váš CRM tým teprve skládá, věrnostní program nemusí čekat: CRM můžeme na přechodnou dobu převzít, spustit první segmenty a kampaně a pak ho předat vašim lidem.

Pokud to teď není priorita, stačí napsat a přestanu se ozývat.

{{sender_first_name}}
```
